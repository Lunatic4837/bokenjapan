Sights in Mitake, Gifu

Mitake, Gifu: things to do, food and stays · Gifu · 24 places

See

  • Daiji-zan Gankō-ji

    Oterayama Gankoji Temple

    #1 ranked in Mitake

    Founded in 815, Gankoji, also called Kani Yakushi, is a historic temple whose gate town became Mitake-juku.

  • Mimi Jinja

    Mimi Shrine

    #2 ranked in Mitake

    This shrine is said to help ear illness because a deaf carpenter was instantly cured after praying here.

  • Oni-no-Ittō Iwa & Renge Iwa

    Oni no Ittoiwa and Rengeiwa

    #3 ranked in Mitake

    Granite rock formations near Mitake known for panoramic views, cherry blossoms and autumn leaves.

  • Mitake Forest

    Mitake Forest and Kinpo Fureai Forest

    #4 ranked in Mitake

    Forest park in Mitake offering seasonal flowers, autumn foliage and panoramic mountain views.

  • Gukeiji

    Daichizan Gukei Zen Temple

    #5 ranked in Mitake

    This Rinzai Myoshinji temple has about 17,000 square meters of grounds and Garyu Sekitei, a dry landscape garden.

  • Fushimi-shuku

    Nakasendo Fushimi-juku

    #8 ranked in Mitake

    Established in 1694 when Tsuchida-juku was abolished, Fushimi-juku bustled with goods near Nimura Port.

  • Mitake-shuku Honjin

    Ontake-juku Honjin

    #9 ranked in Mitake

    Slightly west in the post town, this Edo-period honjin lodged daimyo and court nobles.

  • Minamiyama Park

    Nanzan Park

    #10 ranked in Mitake

    A nature park with a baseball field, pool, tennis courts, a roller slide, and a spring cherry-blossom tunnel.

  • Takeya Merchant House

    Nakasendo Ontake-juku Takeya Merchant House

    #14 ranked in Mitake

    Historic merchant residence built around 1877 on the Nakasendo in Mitake-shuku.

  • Uta-zaka Stone Path

    Utozaka Stone Pavement

    #15 ranked in Mitake

    Out-of-breath travelers sang while climbing this long slope, which may have produced the name Utozaka.

  • Kao Castle Ruins

    Kaodo Castle Ruins

    #16 ranked in Mitake

    Akechi-sho, which included Kaodo, stretched from northeastern Kani to western Mitake and was highlighted by the drama Kirin ga Kuru.

  • Mitake Castle Ruins

    Mitake Castle Ruins

    #17 ranked in Mitake

    Mitake Castle comprises Gongenyama and Honjinyama castles and was reportedly the seat of warrior Oguri Shinano no Kami.

  • Fushimi-shuku Honjin Site

    Fushimi-juku Honjin Site Monument

    #18 ranked in Mitake

    The honjin monument and lattice houses recall the past, beside a marker once noting Owari domain land east of Nishizaka.

  • Izumi Shikibu Grave

    Izumi Shikibu Mausoleum

    #19 ranked in Mitake

    Praised as a passionate Heian poet, Izumi Shikibu is said to have fallen ill here, sought a cure at Oniiwa Onsen, and died.

  • Kamieguchi Castle Ruins

    Kamiedo Castle Ruins

    #20 ranked in Mitake

    A late-15th-century flatland castle first called Edo Castle, later Kamiedo Castle, whose lord plotted against Mori Nagayoshi around 1582.

  • Yamauchi Kasuke Residence Ruins

    Yamauchi Kasuke Residence Site

    #21 ranked in Mitake

    Edo-period sake brewers' residence site; only a stone wall remains, once a Nakasendo rest stop for daimyo and travelers.

  • Ohara Castle Ruins

    Kohara Castle Ruins

    #22 ranked in Mitake

    Small castle remains survive behind Hakusan Shrine in Kohara; the Ogura lords lived west of the shrine and used the castle only in emergencies.

  • Moronogi Pass Observatory

    Moronoki Pass Lookout

    #23 ranked in Mitake

    Said to have been named Gotenba after a rest palace for Princess Kazunomiya; the lookout now serves Nakasendo walkers.

  • Ushinohana Kake Slope

    Ushi no Hanakake Slope

    #24 ranked in Mitake

    Named because the slope was so steep that cows and horses scraped their noses on the ground; a national historic site since 2016.

  • Mitake-no-mori Cherry Trees

    Mitake Forest Cherry Blossoms

    #25 ranked in Mitake

    A park for picnics and birdwatching, with seasonal flowers, autumn leaves, winter snow, and views of Mounts Kinpo, Ena, and Ibuki.

  • Juppongi Rest Station

    Juppongi Tateba

    #26 ranked in Mitake

    Said to have grown from a porters' rest into a Kisoji teahouse stop named for dozens of pines.

  • Utasaka Ichirizuka

    Utaizaka Ichirizuka

    #27 ranked in Mitake

    None of Mitake's three original ichirizuka remain; this one, demolished after an 1908 sale, was restored by local volunteers in 1973.

  • Nakasendo Mitake Kan

    Mitake, Gifu

    Nakasendo Mitake Kan is a visitor facility on the Nakasendo route in Mitake.

  • Oni no Kubizuka

    Mitake, Gifu

    Oni no Kubizuka is a historic stone monument in Mitake.
