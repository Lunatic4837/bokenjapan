Sights in Mizunami, Gifu

Mizunami, Gifu: things to do, food and stays · Gifu · 19 places

See

  • Oniiwa Park

    Autumn Leaves at Oniiwa Park

    #1 ranked in Mizunami

    Granite rock formations and autumn foliage within Hida-Kiso River Quasi-National Park.

  • Oniwa Park

    Oniiwa Park Site of the Oni Legend

    #3 ranked in Mizunami

    Oniwa Park in Mizunami shows large granite boulders contrasting with surrounding greenery inside Hida Kisogawa Quasi-National Park.

  • Mino Kabuki Museum

    Mino Kabuki Museum (Aioiza)

    #4 ranked in Mizunami

    Mino Kabuki Museum in Mizunami displays a relocated Edo-period kabuki theater complete with two hanamichi and a revolving stage.

  • Mizunami Fossil Museum

    Mizunami City Fossil Museum

    #7 ranked in Mizunami

    It stores over 250,000 fossils, displays about 3,000, and offers fossil collecting at an outdoor study site.

  • Ichinose Kota Memorial Art Museum

    Mizunami City Ichinose Hirota Memorial Art Museum

    #9 ranked in Mizunami

    It mainly holds about 300 works by the late Mizunami-born sculptor Ichinose Hirota.

  • World's Largest Mino Ware Komainu

    World's Largest Mino Ware Komainu

    #12 ranked in Mizunami

    Made by 1,000 people to commemorate the city's 35th anniversary, this 3.3-meter Mino ware komainu pair used 15 tons of clay.

  • World's Largest Tea Jar

    World's Largest Tea Jar (Hojo no Tsubo)

    #13 ranked in Mizunami

    This 5.4-meter-high jar, 4 meters in diameter, used 32 tons of clay and is the world's largest one-piece ceramic.

  • Ichinichi Market Hachiman Shrine

    Tsuitachi Shijo Hachimanjinja | Kanko Spot | Gifuken Kanko Koshiki Saito Gifu no Tabi Gaido

    #15 ranked in Mizunami

    Said to be where Toki clan founder Minamoto no Mitsuhira built his residence; a statue of Akechi Mitsuhide stands in the grounds.

  • Grave of Toki Yorisada

    Tomb of Toki Yorisada

    #16 ranked in Mizunami

    Tomb of Toki Yorisada, Nanbokucho-period military governor of Mino, and his family; a road splits the site east and west.

  • Sawa Shrine

    Sakanami Shrine

    #18 ranked in Mizunami

    An old shrine already founded in the Heian period; the Toki clan enshrined Hachiman here, and it was called Hachimangu until the Edo period.

  • Kaigen-in

    Kaigen-in Temple

    #19 ranked in Mizunami

    A Soto Zen temple reputedly founded by Toki Yorimoto; the Kannon, said to come from Jorinji in Toki, is a prefectural Important Cultural Property.

  • Meibaku-ji Gorinto

    Meihakuji Five-Ring Pagoda

    #20 ranked in Mizunami

    Mizunami's largest five-ring pagoda and a city-designated cultural property, thought built from late Kamakura to Nanbokucho times, possibly Toki Yorimoto's tomb.

  • Koto-ku-ji

    Kotokuji Temple

    #21 ranked in Mizunami

    Rinzai temple founded as the Ozato clan's family temple; it holds a Gifu Important Cultural Property statue of Sugawara no Michizane.

  • Nakasendo Biwa Pass

    Mizunami, Gifu

    Nakasendo Biwa Pass is a mountain pass on the Nakasendo trail in Mizunami.

  • Mizunami Ceramics Museum

    Mizunami, Gifu

    Mizunami Ceramics Museum is a ceramics museum in Mizunami.

  • Ryugin Gorge

    Mizunami, Gifu

    Ryugin Gorge is a river gorge in Mizunami.

  • Former Morikawa Kun Yukiie House

    Mizunami, Gifu

    Former Morikawa Kun Yukiie House is a historic residence in Mizunami.

  • Oni no Itto Rock and Renge Rock

    Mizunami, Gifu

    Oni no Itto Rock and Renge Rock are natural rock formations in Mizunami.

  • Nakasendo Oshuku-juku

    Mizunami, Gifu

    Nakasendo Oshuku-juku is a post town on the Nakasendo trail in Mizunami.
