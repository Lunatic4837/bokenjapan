Sights in Sekigahara, Gifu

Sekigahara, Gifu: things to do, food and stays · Gifu · 38 places

See

  • Sekigahara Battlefield

    Sekigahara Historic Battlefield

    #1 ranked in Sekigahara

    Amid the fields, stone monuments and Tokugawa and Ishida crest flags stand where Sekigahara fighting was especially fierce.

  • Mount Ibuki Driveway

    Mount Ibuki Driveway

    #2 ranked in Sekigahara

    A 17 km automobile-only road spanning Shiga and Gifu climbs near the 1,377-meter summit of Mount Ibuki, one of Japan's 100 famous mountains.

  • Gifu Sekigahara Battlefield Memorial Museum

    Gifu Sekigahara Historic Battlefield Memorial Museum

    #3 ranked in Sekigahara

    Opened in October 2020 in Sekigahara, this hands-on museum uses the latest technology so visitors can understand the whole 1600 battle.

  • Sekigahara War Land

    Sekigahara War Land

    #4 ranked in Sekigahara

    Asano Shoun's 200-plus concrete figures recreate the Sekigahara battle, including Tokugawa Ieyasu, Ishida Mitsunari, gun squads, and foot soldiers.

  • Mount Ibuki Driveway Autumn Foliage

    Mount Ibuki Driveway Autumn Leaves

    #5 ranked in Sekigahara

    Mount Ibuki is the recommended autumn-leaf spot in Sekigahara; trail kusamomiji maples start coloring from around early October.

  • Tokugawa Ieyasu Final Camp Site

    Tokugawa Ieyasu's Final Camp Site and Shogiba

    #6 ranked in Sekigahara

    Around 11 a.m. on the day of the battle, Tokugawa Ieyasu moved his headquarters here from Momokubariyama to face Ishida Mitsunari's camp.

  • Sekigahara Battlefield Decisive Ground

    Sekigahara Historic Battlefield Decisive Battle Site

    #7 ranked in Sekigahara

    On the morning of September 15, 1600, Kobayakawa Hideaki's betrayal turned the Western advantage into an Eastern Army victory.

  • Otani Yoshitsugu Monument

    Otani Yoshitsugu Memorial Monument

    #8 ranked in Sekigahara

    Erected in 1940 by the Fuwa Historic Sites Preservation Society, it presents Otani Yoshitsugu's end as a model of bushido.

  • Sekigahara Limestone Cave

    Sekigahara Limestone Cave

    #11 ranked in Sekigahara

    The cave stays about 15 degrees, with stalagmites, rainbow trout in spring water, and Paleozoic fossils; the 518-meter route takes about 20 minutes.

  • Sasao-yama Ishida Mitsunari Camp Site

    Ishida Mitsunari Camp Site on Sasaoyama

    #12 ranked in Sekigahara

    Ishida Mitsunari, Western Army commander, camped on Sasaoyama the day before the battle to overlook Sekigahara and hold the Hokkoku Kaido.

  • Myo-oji Temple

    Myooji Temple

    #15 ranked in Sekigahara

    Founded in 1360 by Nagae Shigekage for his late mother, it is the prefecture's oldest Soto Zen temple.

  • Hosokawa Tadaoki Jin-ato

    Hosokawa Tadaoki Camp Site

    #16 ranked in Sekigahara

    Hosokawa Tadaoki, one of Sen no Rikyu's seven disciples, joined the Eastern Army at Sekigahara from the start.

  • Momohai-yama Tokugawa Ieyasu Jin-ato

    Momokubariyama, Tokugawa Ieyasu's First Camp Site

    #17 ranked in Sekigahara

    Eastern Army commander-in-chief Tokugawa Ieyasu deployed at Momokubariyama on the morning of September 15, 1600.

  • Sekigahara Kessenjo Kaisenchi

    Sekigahara Historic Battlefield Opening Site

    #18 ranked in Sekigahara

    The Battle of Sekigahara opened here when Fukushima Masanori volleyed after the Ii unit fired early.

  • Otani Yoshitsugu Grave and Yuasa Gonsuke Grave

    Graves of Otani Yoshitsugu and Yuasa Gosuke

    #19 ranked in Sekigahara

    Otani Yoshitsugu's grave stands quietly with aide Yuasa Gosuke's, a 15-minute climb from Hiratsuka Tamehiro's monument.

  • Matsudaira Tadachika Ii Naomasa Jin-ato

    Matsudaira Tadayoshi and Ii Naomasa Camp Site

    #20 ranked in Sekigahara

    Ii Naomasa wanted his son-in-law Matsudaira Tadayoshi, Ieyasu's fourth son, to earn merit in his first battle.

  • Otani Yoshitsugu Jin-ato

    Otani Yoshitsugu Camp Site

    #21 ranked in Sekigahara

    Otani's camp, begun about ten days before the battle above Wakamiya Hachimangu, had dry moats on both sides.

  • Matsuo-yama Castle Ruins Kobayakawa Hideaki Jin-ato

    Matsuo Yama Castle Ruins and Kobayakawa Hideaki Camp Site

    #22 ranked in Sekigahara

    Matsuo Yama Castle is famous as the mountain castle where Kobayakawa Hideaki deployed his forces.

  • Sekigahara Town History and Folklore Museum

    Sekigahara Town History and Folklore Learning Center

    #23 ranked in Sekigahara

    Renewed in October 2020, it focuses on Sekigahara Town's local history with educational and hands-on elements.

  • Shimazu Yoshihiro Jin-ato

    Shimazu Yoshihiro Camp Site (Shimazu Retreat Site)

    #24 ranked in Sekigahara

    The Shimazu Yoshihiro camp site lies behind Shinmei Shrine, near the center of the Western Army positions.

  • Okayama Beacon Hill

    Kuroda Nagamasa and Takenaka Shigekado Camp Site / Okayama (Maruyama) Beacon Site

    #25 ranked in Sekigahara

    On 164-meter Okayama hill, Takenaka Shigekado and Kuroda Nagamasa camped and raised Sekigahara's opening beacon.

  • Myojin no Mori

    Sekigahara Myojin Forest

    #27 ranked in Sekigahara

    North of the old battlefield beside Mount Ibuki, Myojin Forest offers seasonal flowers and wonderful views.

  • Higashi Kubizuka

    Higashi Kubizuka

    #28 ranked in Sekigahara

    Just west of JR Sekigahara Station, Higashi Kubizuka is in a broadleaf forest beyond a large vermilion gate.

  • Yamauchi Kazutoyo Jin-ato

    Yamauchi Kazutoyo Camp Site

    #29 ranked in Sekigahara

    Along the old Nakasendo, the camp site is marked by a large pine and the Yamauchi oak-leaf war banner.

  • Shima Sakon Jin-ato

    Shima Sakon Camp Site

    #30 ranked in Sekigahara

    Called too good for Mitsunari, Shima Sakon camped at the foot of Mount Sasao and repeatedly repelled Eastern Army attacks.

  • Honda Tadakatsu Jin-ato

    Honda Tadakatsu Camp Site

    #33 ranked in Sekigahara

    Honda Tadakatsu, one of Tokugawa's Four Heavenly Kings, was a brave, strategic general also loved by his domain's people.

  • Ukita Hideie Jin-ato

    Nantenmanzan Ukita Hideie Camp Site

    #34 ranked in Sekigahara

    Tenman Shrine stands at the foot of cedar-covered Nantenmanzan, where Ukita Hideie deployed about 17,000 Western troops.

  • Shoren-ji Yatsufusa-ume

    Yatsufusa Plum at Shorenji Temple

    #35 ranked in Sekigahara

    Shorenji Temple in Hirai, Imasu, has the Yatsufusa plum, a natural monument designated by the town and prefecture.

  • Tanaka Yoshimasa Jin-ato

    Tanaka Yoshimasa Camp Site

    #36 ranked in Sekigahara

    The monument stands beside Tokugawa Ieyasu's final camp and was moved to Jinbano in 1987.

  • Fukushima Masanori Jin-ato

    Fukushima Masanori Camp Site

    #37 ranked in Sekigahara

    His camp remains at Kasuga Shrine with the 800-year-old Tsukimi-no-miya cedar depicted in the Sekigahara battle screen.

  • Konishi Yukinaga Camp Site

    Hokutenmanzan Konishi Yukinaga Camp Site

    #38 ranked in Sekigahara

    Konishi Yukinaga deployed about 6,000 Western Army troops at Hokutenmanzan and raised a signal fire for his allies.

  • Hiratsuka Tamekuni Monument

    Hiratsuka Tamehiro Monument

    #39 ranked in Sekigahara

    It stands east of the road downhill from the Otani Yoshitsugu camp site.

  • Nishi Kubizuka

    West Head Mound

    #40 ranked in Sekigahara

    Takenaka Shigekado buried soldiers killed in the Battle of Sekigahara here on Tokugawa Ieyasu's orders.

  • Satsuma-ike Pond

    Satsuma Pond

    #41 ranked in Sekigahara

    A small pond used militarily by Shimazu Yoshihiro's troops, said never to have dried since ancient times.

  • Todo Takatora & Kyogoku Takatomo Camp Site

    Todo Takatora and Kyogoku Takatomo Camp Site

    #42 ranked in Sekigahara

    Todo Takatora and Kyogoku Takatomo reportedly camped on present-day Sekigahara Junior High School grounds.

  • Wakizaka Yasuharu Camp Site

    Wakisaka Yasuharu Camp Site

    #44 ranked in Sekigahara

    It lies at the foot of Mt. Matsuo, between the Meishin Expressway and Kurochi River.

  • Fujikogawa Fireflies

    Fujiko River Fireflies

    #45 ranked in Sekigahara

    From early to mid-June, Genji fireflies can be seen around Fujiko River, sometimes hundreds at once.

    Historic grave located in Sekigahara.
