Sights

  • Natsukashi no Aji Handmade Workshop

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-38-3787

    On the first floor, sweets and side dishes such as Kushimochi, Sesame Kamasu, traditional-flavored Kyabamochi, Sanshokumochi, and "Multi-grain starch syrup" certified by a food master are sold.

  • Katsuraan

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-38-2125

    A soba restaurant located at the foot of Tendaiji Temple. The temple was named by the honorary chief priest, Jakucho Setouchi, from the many Katsura trees surrounding the temple.

  • Zakkoku (Mixed Grains)

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-22-4362

    The Ninohe region is located at the northern tip of Iwate Prefecture, and has been actively cultivated for millet since ancient times.

  • Shiki no Sato Workshop

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-23-7148

    When you open the shoji door at the front, you will find a renovated table and chairs.

  • Kita no Chocolate Factory & Store 2door

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-22-2222

    2door is a name that comes from the meaning of "Ninohe" and the tradition and fusion of Nanbu rice crackers and chocolate.

  • Grain Starch Syrup (Zakkoku Mizuame)

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-38-3787

    A rustic flavor made by stuffing millet and barley bean sprouts over a period of about two weeks.

  • Nanbu Senbei Iwateya

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-23-6311

    A traditional rice cracker made from wheat, sesame, and peanuts.

  • Nanbu Bijin Sake

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-23-3133

    This sake is brewed using the pure, medium-hard water flowing from Masen Gorge, and polished to an average rice polishing rate of 54%, using prefecture-produced rice such as Miyama Nishiki and Ginginga, using Nanbu Ryu hand-brewing techniques.

  • Ninohe History and Folklore Museum

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-23-9120

    It exhibits artifacts excavated from the Horino ruins, ethnic materials such as daily utensils, and artifacts left by local ancestors.

  • Nanbu Senbei Ishibashi Senbei

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-38-2636

    A long-established Nanbu rice cracker shop in Ninohe City that has been in business for 51 years.

  • Fureai Ninohe

    Ninohe, Iwate · 0195-23-0288

    There is a wide selection of fresh fruits and vegetables, including "Natsukoi" cherries, which are harvested in early July every year, "Tenpo", which has a chewy texture that contains sesame seeds and perilla, organically grown cereals, and cooking tomatoes.
