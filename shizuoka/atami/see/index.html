Sights in Atami, Shizuoka

Atami, Shizuoka: things to do, food and stays · Shizuoka · 11 places

See

  • Kiunkaku

    起雲閣

    #3 ranked in Atami

    Kiunkaku, one of Atami's three great villas, joins a Japanese wing with a Western wing of stained glass and a Roman-style bath.

  • Atami Castle

    熱海城

    #4 ranked in Atami

    An observation keep on Nishikigaura at 160 meters, with Atami and Sagami Bay views and a nightscape called the million-dollar view.

  • ACAO FOREST

    ACAO FOREST

    ACAO Forest

    #5 ranked in Atami

  • Atami Plum Garden

    熱海梅園

    #6 ranked in Atami

    Atami Plum Garden has over 450 plum trees of about 60 varieties, known for Japan's earliest plum blossoms and latest autumn leaves.

  • Omiya no Matsu and Konjiki Yasha Monument

    お宮の松・金色夜叉の碑

    #7 ranked in Atami

    A coastal memorial to Ozaki Koyo's Konjiki Yasha, with the second Omiya pine, the first tree's stump, and bronze statues.

  • Atami Sanbichi

    熱海サンビーチ

    #8 ranked in Atami

    This roughly 400-meter sandy beach has palms, pale-blue night lighting, and is a main venue for Atami's sea fireworks.

  • Ajiro Onsen Beach (Onawa)

    網代温泉海水浴場（大縄）

    #9 ranked in Atami

    A small shallow artificial beach near Ajiro fishing port, with very calm waves and free seasonal cold showers.

  • Izusan Shrine

    伊豆山神社

    #10 ranked in Atami

    Izusan Shrine, said to be the origin of the name Izu, is known for Yoritomo and Masako's secret meetings and matchmaking blessings.

  • Kinomiya Shrine

    來宮神社

    #11 ranked in Atami

    A shrine a short walk from Kinomiya Station, with a camphor tree over 2,100 years old and about 24 meters around.

  • Nagahama Beach

    長浜海水浴場

    #12 ranked in Atami

    An artificial beach about 400 meters long in Atami's Taga district, with calm, shallow water suited to families.

  • MOA Art Museum

    MOA美術館

    #13 ranked in Atami

    An art museum on a hill behind Atami Station, offering Sagami Bay views and an outdoor Japanese garden called Cha no Niwa.
