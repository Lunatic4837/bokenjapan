Sights in Shimada, Shizuoka

Shimada, Shizuoka: things to do, food and stays · Shizuoka · 9 places

See

  • Nationally Designated Shimada-juku Oi River Crossing Site

    国指定島田宿大井川川越遺跡

    #1 ranked in Shimada

    This historic site preserves the Tokaido Oi River crossing, where people were carried across, with period river offices and porter lodgings.

  • Oi Shrine

    大井神社

    #2 ranked in Shimada

    Oi Shrine, long revered as a water guardian and for safe childbirth, holds the triennial Shimada Obi Festival, one of Japan's three unusual festivals.

  • Shimada City Rose Hill Park

    島田市ばらの丘公園

    #3 ranked in Shimada

    About 360 rose varieties from around the world fill this leading prefectural garden, with spring and autumn festivals, photo spots, and rose soft-serve.

  • Old Tokaido Stone Pavement (Kanaya Slope)

    旧東海道石畳（金谷坂）

    #4 ranked in Shimada

    Late-Edo stone pavement on Kanaya Slope of the Old Tokaido; 430 meters were restored in 1991 amid Makinohara tea fields.

  • Makinohara Great Tea Plantation

    牧之原大茶園

    #5 ranked in Shimada

    About 5,000 hectares of tea fields spanning Makinohara, Shimada, and Kikugawa, begun in 1869 by former samurai and river porters.

  • Seven Bends of Uyama

    鵜山の七曲り

    #6 ranked in Shimada

    The Oi River bends seven times through rare terrain in Kawanehon, a prefectural natural monument best seen from the highway overlook.

  • Tennozan Park

    天王山公園

    #7 ranked in Shimada

    A seasonal park on a 168-meter hill about ten minutes from Ieyama Station, known for cherry blossoms and handmade winter lights.

  • Horai Bridge

    蓬莱橋

    #10 ranked in Shimada

    Guinness-certified world's longest wooden footbridge, 897.4 meters across the Oi River, built for Makinohara tea fields.

  • Fujinokuni Tea Capital Museum

    ふじのくに茶の都ミュージアム

    #11 ranked in Shimada

    Offers hands-on tea classes, including stone-mill matcha grinding, tea ceremony with sweets, and brewing Shizuoka tea.
