Things to do in Shonai, Yamagata

Shonai · Yamagata · 8 places

Do

  • Gassan

    月山

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0235-62-4321

    One of three Dewa Sanzan peaks of mountain worship, where people pray for happiness in a past life. With Mount Haguro and Mount Yudono, the pilgrimage is called a journey of rebirth.

  • Kart Soleil Mogamigawa

    カートソレイユ最上川

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0234-42-2282

    Racing kart course 1,063 meters long in Mogami River scenery, for full races and family use. Single-seat karts reach 70 km/h, and the felt speed is said to be about twice that.

  • Kiyokawa Hachiro Memorial Museum

    清河八郎記念館

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0234-57-2104

    Displays relics of Kiyokawa Hachiro, a forerunner of the imperial restoration movement with a major role in the Meiji Restoration. Kiyokawa Shrine and Kanki-ji, with gravestones of Hachiro and his wife Oren, are nearby.

  • Rokufuchi and Seba Erosion-Control Dams

    六渕・瀬場 砂防堰堤

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0234-56-2213

    Built in 1945-1954, Rokufuchi and Seba are cobblestone-concrete erosion-control dams with curved spillways, now hard to restore. An inscription is carved on the back of the Seba monument.

  • Tateyama Park

    楯山公園

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0234-56-2217

    Park on the former Karikawa Castle site, opened in 1915 to commemorate the emperor's enthronement. About 200 Somei-Yoshino cherry trees stand on a low hill with views over the Shonai Plain.

  • Sabo Museum

    砂防資料館(国土交通省立谷沢川砂防出張所内)

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0234-56-2050

    Completed in 1988 to compile techniques, materials, and information from 50 years of erosion-control work under the Shinjo Construction Office.

  • Shonai Town Gallery Onsen Machiyu

    庄内町ギャラリー温泉 町湯

    Shonai, Yamagata

    Onsen that pairs hot-spring bathing with a gallery of artworks and products. Machiya-style exterior, with baths and an open-air bath set around a courtyard, plus a sauna.

  • Kame-no-o no Sato Museum

    亀ノ尾の里資料館

    Shonai, Yamagata · 0234-44-2162

    Displays farm tools and folk implements honoring Abe Kameji, who developed Kamenoo rice, a root of Sasanishiki and Koshihikari. Attached to Amarume No. 4 Community Center; a hina-doll exhibition runs in spring.
